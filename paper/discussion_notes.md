# Discussion / interpretation notes — MCA hunting & fishing paper

Working notes to carry field context into the write-up. Neutral framing throughout:
hunting and fishing are legitimate landowner resource use; report as records.

## Governing caution — the baseline was never even
- Animal distribution across the MCA was **never uniform**: elevation, forest type
  and terrain vary widely between zones, so different zones naturally hold different
  fauna to begin with.
- Therefore **between-zone differences in what is hunted reflect the naturally uneven
  baseline distribution first** — not, by default, a human/hunting effect. No zone is
  a baseline for another.
- Inference about *hunting effects* should come from **within-zone change over time**
  (the age-recall abundance and frequency data), not from cross-zone comparisons of
  which species are present.
- Cross-zone results are best presented as **description of what each area holds and
  uses**, tied to habitat/elevation — not as a gradient of impact.

## Zone 6 — elevation, not depletion
- Zone 6 includes **high peaks / montane forest on the Hydrographers Range**; other
  survey zones lack that high-elevation habitat.
- **Tree kangaroo** and **long-beaked echidna (*Zaglossus*)** appear in the Zone 6
  hunted fauna (TK ~62% of Zone 6 hunters; echidna named in 10 of ~44 Zone 6
  records) and are essentially absent from the other zones' records.
- **Interpretation: this is habitat/elevation availability, NOT hunting depletion.**
  These species are elevation-restricted; zones without high peaks are outside
  their range, so their absence there is expected regardless of hunting. Do not
  frame the lower zones as "depleted" of TK/echidna, and do not read the
  between-zone difference as a defaunation gradient.
- Analytical consequence: interpret elevation-restricted species (TK, echidna)
  against which zones hold the montane habitat. Use per-zone elevation/altitude as
  a covariate if available. Species present across zones (pig, cassowary, cuscus,
  bandicoot, etc.) are compared on their own terms, separately.
- **Update (data to 4 Sept):** the pattern holds across **three** high-elevation
  zones. Tree kangaroo: Zone 6 20/32 hunters, Zone 10 10/26, and **Zone 9 24/40**;
  echidna concentrated in the same three zones and near-absent in Zones 7A/7B/8/3.
  Consistent with elevation/habitat availability, not depletion.
- **Zone → mountain-range mapping (from WT; do not guess these):**
  - **Zone 9 fringes the Sibium Range.**
  - Owalama Range: WT field note that it may also hold tree kangaroos — still
    UNCONFIRMED and not yet tied to a surveyed zone. Treat as a hypothesis to
    check, not a finding; do not state as fact.
  - Zones 6 and 10 reach the high peaks (Hydrographers Range noted earlier for
    Zone 6). **Confirm the exact range for each zone with the team before naming
    a range in any output — never infer a zone's mountain range from the data.**

## Codebook reminders (also in CLAUDE.md)
- Fishing method `dynamite` = local name for a **plant root fish poison**, not an
  explosive; gloss accordingly; single record so far.
- `Pilai` = monitor lizard.

## Framing
- The community holds detailed knowledge of, and access to, fauna (incl. rare/
  elevation-restricted species) on their own land. Present species records and takes
  as neutral data; conservation status is a factual attribute, never a judgement.
